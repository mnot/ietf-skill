# Wiretapping — RFC 2804

**Text:** `rfc2804.txt`

## Does it fire?

The design lets a party other than the endpoints obtain content or metadata, or makes an endpoint
report on its own user. Check the definition before concluding it fires — see below.

## Where to look

- **§3** — the definition, in four conditions. This is where most candidate findings die, and
  reading it first saves building an argument you will then have to kill.
- **§1** — the IETF's position, including the statement that it is the wrong forum for features
  arising from the laws of individual countries, and that mechanisms enabling interception should
  be openly described.
- **§4** — why no moral position is taken, and the observation that tools built for one purpose get
  used for others.

## What this lens asks

- Does a third party obtain the communication, and does the sender know?
- Is the capability designed in, or merely a consequence of someone being trusted anyway?
- If the draft depends on national law or a jurisdictional authority, is that dependency stated?
- Where the capability exists but is not the purpose, is it at least described?

## Firing, versus only looking like it

**This lens is built and then killed more often than any other, and §3 is why.** Its four
conditions include delivery to a *third* party without the sending party's knowledge. A provider
that could substitute a key, or a server that sees a request it was deliberately sent, generally
fails that test — the party is not third, or the sender knows.

Two things usually survive when the definition does not:

- **§1's venue argument**, where a design takes its content from a jurisdiction. That is an
  institutional finding, and often a strong one.
- **§4's dual-use observation**, as support for a concern about scope or applicability — not as an
  accusation about intent.

Recording that you opened §3 and it did not fit is worth a line; the next reviewer will otherwise
build the same argument again.

## What it does not cover

Observation by a party the user contacted, which is RFC 6973, and monitoring at scale, which is
RFC 7258.
