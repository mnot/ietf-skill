# Filtering and blocking — RFC 7754

**Text:** `rfc7754.txt`

## Does it fire?

Something in the design decides whether a communication proceeds — or the design creates a point
at which a third party could make that decision for others.

## Where to look

- **§6** — the conclusion, including the preference for filtering at the endpoint. The section most
  often cited, and the yardstick for network-side enforcement.
- **§3.1–3.4** — who sets blocking policy, and for what purposes. §3.2 is useful because it lists
  legitimate purposes, which stops the lens becoming an objection to filtering as such.
- **§4.3** — rendezvous-based blocking, when the design has a lookup or discovery step that
  someone could suppress.
- **§4.1.4** — the counterproductive case: a blocking mechanism that helps users locate what was
  blocked.
- **§4.4** — endpoint-based blocking and its properties.

## What this lens asks

- Who decides, and does the affected party know a decision was made?
- Is the enforcement point the endpoint, or somewhere in the network that cannot see context?
- If the mechanism is suppressed, can a participant tell suppression from absence?
- Does a transparency or appeal path exist, and does it reach the party affected?

## Firing, versus only looking like it

This lens is about *deliberate blocking of communications by a party other than the endpoints*. It
does not fire on a protocol's own internal arbitration — a node ignoring a misbehaving peer, an
algorithm-negotiation failure, a rate limit. Those are ordinary protocol behaviour and reaching for
RFC 7754 there overstates.

In practice the actionable content usually turns out to be the same as some other finding — most
often "the design provides no way to distinguish failure from suppression", which belongs with the
response-semantics issue. Folding it there and recording that the lens was opened is the honest
outcome, and it happens often.

## What it does not cover

Interception, which is RFC 2804. Filtering the user chose and controls is in scope but rarely a
finding.
